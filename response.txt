NVDA:

Nvidia is evolving from a GPU designer into a full-stack AI infrastructure
provider. The company is expanding its ecosystem through the acquisition of
SchedMD to control the Slurm workload manager and a $2 billion investment in
Marvell for custom silicon and networking. New hardware platforms, including
Blackwell and the upcoming Vera Rubin, are being deployed for training,
inference, and agentic AI workloads.

The company is also diversifying into edge AI with Firefly Aerospace for lunar
orbit analytics and joining Project Glasswing for cybersecurity. Despite its
dominance, Nvidia faces pressure from hyperscalers developing custom chips and
geopolitical tensions regarding a truce between the U.S. and Iran.

Keypoints: * Nvidia is expanding its full-stack AI platform through strategic
partnerships. * The company acquired SchedMD to gain control of the open-source
Slurm software. * New Rubin and Vera Rubin platforms are targeting agentic AI
workloads. * A $2 billion investment in Marvell focuses on custom silicon and
networking. * The company is deploying Jetson edge AI platforms for lunar orbit
analytics. * Geopolitical tensions with Iran have impacted short-term stock
volatility.

AMD:

AMD is positioning itself as a primary competitor to Nvidia by focusing on AI
inference and agentic AI. The company has secured major GPU deployments with
OpenAI and Meta Platforms and is leveraging its leadership in data center CPUs
to meet the reasoning demands of AI agents. The launch of the MI450 and Helios
platform in late 2026 is expected to shift AMD toward system-level revenue.

AMD is also a key backer of the UALink 2.0 specification to promote open, multi-
vendor connectivity for AI accelerators. While the company shows strong growth
in its data center segment, it faces risks from hyperscalers shifting toward
proprietary Arm-based chips.

Keypoints: * AMD is targeting growth in AI inference and agentic AI workloads. *
The company secured large GPU capacity deals with OpenAI and Meta Platforms. *
MI450 and Helios platforms are expected to launch in the second half of 2026. *
AMD is a key backer of the open UALink 2.0 specification for accelerators. * The
company is the current leader in the data center CPU market. * Hyperscaler
shifts to Arm-based chips present a risk to x86 CPU demand.
